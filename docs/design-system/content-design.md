# Content Design

> Built from an interview on 2026-07-26. This sits between voice (`../brand/voice-and-tone.md`) and layout (`ux-patterns.md`). It covers how content is structured and sequenced for scanning and comprehension.

## Guiding principle: guidance, not a template
These are patterns to reach for, not a formula to apply mechanically. Vary structure to fit the piece. A site where every page follows the same rigid skeleton reads as formulaic or machine-written. Keep it human: let the content shape the page, not the other way around.

## Structure content BLUF
Front-load the answer.
- Lead with the outcome, the fix, or the key point. Then explain, show and support it.
- A reader who stops after the first screen should still get the value.
- Exception: reflective and opinion pieces may open with a hook and build, as noted in the writing style guide. Use judgment.

## Make it scannable
Standard aids, used where they genuinely help:
- **Descriptive subheadings.** Headings say what the section delivers, not "Introduction" or "Overview". A reader should follow the argument by reading headings alone.
- **TL;DR for long posts.** For posts over 800 words, a one or two line summary at the top.
- **Key takeaways.** A short summary box for dense or long pieces, so the core points are captured in one place.
- Bulleted lists for steps and options, prose for reasoning. Do not turn everything into bullets.

## Keep depth inline
- No collapsibles or accordions. Show the content, do not hide it behind a click.
- If a section is truly optional, say so in text or move it lower, but keep it on the page.
- Progressive disclosure happens through order (most important first), not through hidden panels.

## Sequencing by page
Guidance, not rigid order:
- **Article:** answer or hook first, then the build, then a real takeaway. Related content and one CTA after the body.
- **Project or case study:** outcome first, then problem, constraints, decisions and what was learned.
- **Home:** who this is for and the strongest proof first, then recent work and current focus.
- **About:** a human, editorial intro, then what you cover and why to trust it.

## Writing for comprehension
- One idea per paragraph. Short paragraphs for reading on screens.
- Concrete over abstract. Show a number, a snippet or an example.
- Meaningful link text, never "click here". The link text says where it goes.
- Define a term the first time it appears, then use it plainly.

## Accessibility of content
- Headings nest in order (h1, then h2, then h3). Do not skip levels for styling.
- Every image that carries meaning has alt text. Decorative images have empty alt.
- Link text makes sense out of context.
- Do not rely on color alone to carry meaning.

## Anti-formula checklist
Before publishing, a quick gut check:
- Does this read like a person wrote it, or like a filled-in template?
- Did I vary the structure to fit this piece?
- Is every subhead, TL;DR or box actually useful here, or added by habit?
- Would I cut any section that only exists to hit a pattern?
